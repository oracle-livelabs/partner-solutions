# Dutch safeguarding research boundary

Checked 25 September 2026. These sources inform fictional characteristics, not claims about any person or care business in the demo.

- [Municipality of Amsterdam — Ouderen financieel veilig](https://www.amsterdam.nl/ouderen-financieel-veilig/): distinguishes external doorstep scams from financial abuse by someone known to or relied upon by the older person. Describes unauthorised use of money/property and safeguards around limited payment access and financial oversight. Use this to motivate bounded permissions, receipt questions and dependency-sensitive dialogue; do not reproduce a real victim's story.
- [IGJ / IKZ — Signalen fraude in de zorg 2022](https://www.igj.nl/documenten/2023/07/27/signalen-fraude-in-de-zorg-2022), published 27 July 2023: explicitly reports suspicions of care fraud, not established fraud. Keep a care-funding branch separate from personal-property or shopping-payment concerns, and retain that distinction in summaries and AI reviews.

Not incorporated as dataset facts: the supplied €21,000/93-year-old example, a 2018 prevalence estimate, current Amsterdam prevalence, aggregate “billions” estimates, real providers or offenders. These need their own precise source/date/denominator if requested for a research brief. Old national estimates are not current local counts.

Authenticity here means credible process: gradual concerns, a resident's choice to seek help, separately recorded permission, redacted documents, incomplete settlement information, lawful help and unresolved explanations. Do not introduce PINs, bank account numbers, exact care schedules, exploitable absence windows or instructions for committing abuse. Needing care does not imply impaired memory or lack of decision-making capacity.

The training story is not legal advice, a prevalence model or a real safeguarding assessment.
